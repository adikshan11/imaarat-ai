# Reference Library

They are retrieved as context next to the underwriting guidelines (G1 to G12) and never change the deterministic score. Authority is one of: official (government or regulator), standard (Bureau of Indian Standards), industry (insurer or reinsurer), research (papers and preprints) and news (press reports, non-authoritative context only). Figures are as stated by the source on the date shown. Sentences starting "Our reading" are our interpretation, not the source's. ## R1. Standard fire products for micro and small businesses

Source: IRDAI, Guidelines on Bharat Sookshma Udyam Suraksha and Bharat Laghu Udyam Suraksha, effective 1 April 2021 (text as reproduced by Insurance Times on bimabazaar.com). https://bimabazaar.com/journal-books/insurance-articles/guidelines-for-fire-and-allied-perils-cover-for-standard-products-for-micro-businesses-and-small-businesses-bharat-sookshma-udyam-suraksha-and-bharat-laghu-udyam-suraksha
Authority: official

From 1 April 2021 every fire insurer had to offer two standard products for fresh and renewal business. Bharat Sookshma Udyam Suraksha covers a total value at risk of up to Rs 5 crore at one location when the policy starts; Bharat Laghu Udyam Suraksha covers more than Rs 5 crore and up to Rs 50 crore. Value at risk is everything at the location: building, fittings and fixtures, plant and machinery, stock and other contents. If the value crosses a threshold during the year the policy runs to expiry and the right product is issued at renewal. Building, plant and machinery and furniture are insured on reinstatement value, never market value. Terrorism cover is built in at the rate of the Indian Market Terrorism Risk Insurance Pool. Insuring only part of a building is not allowed. A site with no manufacturing or storage for 30 days or more is a silent risk. Clauses 3, 4, 11 to 14.

## R2. Bharat Griha Raksha for homes

Source: IRDAI, Guidelines introducing the standard product Bharat Griha Raksha, effective 1 April 2021 (text as reproduced on bimabazaar.com). https://bimabazaar.com/insurance-news-and-information/insurance-news/irda-in-india/guidelines-for-fire-and-allied-perils-cover-for-dwellings-introduction-of-a-standard-product-bharat-griha-raksha
Authority: official

Bharat Griha Raksha replaced the old tariff fire policy for dwellings, for building and contents, whatever the sum insured. Policies can run for up to 10 years. Loss of rent, rent for alternative accommodation, debris removal and professional fees are built in, and so is terrorism at the pool rate. The building sum insured is the carpet area times the declared construction cost per unit area; it can be higher but not lower. On long-term policies the building sum insured rises by 10% a year, up to 100%, at no extra premium. General contents default to 20% of the building sum insured, capped at Rs 10 lakh, unless the buyer opts out. Clauses 2, 8, 9, 11 and 12.

## R3. IRDAI master circular on general insurance products, 2024

Source: IRDAI, Master Circular on IRDAI (Insurance Products) Regulations 2024 for general insurance, June 2024, reference IRDAI/NL/MSTCIR/MISC/90/06/2024 (text as reproduced by taxguru.in). https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/master-circular-reforms-general-insurance-business.html
Authority: official

The circular moves general insurance from rule-based to principle-based regulation, but insurers may still not withdraw or refuse the old standard products. Retail products must say whether the average (underinsurance) clause applies and how much of it is waived, and every retail policy comes with a Customer Information Sheet on scope, basis of sum insured, exclusions, deductibles and warranties. Non-motor losses of Rs 1 lakh or more need a registered surveyor, who reports within 15 days; the insurer then decides within 7 days, except on reinstatement-value property policies. Where several policies cover one risk no contribution clause applies, and underinsurance is judged on all sums insured together. Homeowners must be offered add-ons such as flood, cyclone, earthquake, landslide, rockslide and terrorism. Pricing should reflect exposure and catastrophe experience. The 2021 standard-product guidelines are listed as repealed. Chapter I paragraphs 6, 7, 9, 10, 14 and 15; Chapter III; Annex C.

## R4. What Bharat Laghu Udyam Suraksha covers in practice

Source: ACKO, Bharat Laghu Udyam Suraksha product page (undated). https://www.acko.com/fire-insurance/bharat-laghu-udyam-suraksha/
Authority: industry

An insurer's description of the Laghu product for property worth more than Rs 5 crore and up to Rs 50 crore at one location. Covered: fire including spontaneous heating, earthquake, storm, cyclone, flood, lightning and landslide; impact by vehicles, aircraft, falling trees or walls; riot, vandalism and terrorism; sprinkler leakage and bursting or overflow of tanks and pipes; and theft within 7 days of an insured event. Built-in limits include additions and alterations up to 15% of the sum insured, temporary removal of stock up to 10%, start-up costs up to Rs 5 lakh, professional fees up to 5% of the claim and debris removal up to 2%. Underinsurance is waived up to 15%. Exclusions include an electrical machine's own short circuit or self-heating, cold-storage temperature change, pollution, consequential loss, war and nuclear causes.

## R5. What Bharat Sookshma Udyam Suraksha covers in practice

Source: ACKO, Bharat Sookshma Udyam Suraksha product page (undated). https://www.acko.com/fire-insurance/bharat-sookshma-udyam-suraksha/
Authority: industry

An insurer's description of the Sookshma product for assets of up to Rs 5 crore at one location at inception, for one year. Floater cover for stock kept at several locations is built in; declaration cover is an add-on. The fire peril includes explosion and implosion, and natural events listed are earthquake, storm, cyclone, flood and landslide. Start-up expenses are covered up to Rs 1 lakh, lower than the Rs 5 lakh under Laghu. Underinsurance is waived up to 15%, and the exclusions match the Laghu product.

## R6. Sookshma product: sum insured basis and contents limits

Source: Bajaj General Insurance, Bharat Sookshma Udyam Suraksha Policy page (undated). https://www.bajajgeneralinsurance.com/commercial-insurance/bharat-sookshma-udyam-suraksha-policy.html
Authority: industry

Another insurer's summary of the Sookshma product. It covers fire, lightning, explosion, earthquake, storm, flood, riot, strike and malicious damage, and names tsunami and landslide among natural perils. A shortfall in the sum insured of up to 15% does not reduce a claim. Terrorism is standard, not a separate add-on. The sum insured is set on reinstatement or replacement value. Specific contents have their own limits, for example about Rs 50,000 for documents and money and Rs 5 lakh for computer data. Pledged or mortgaged property needs the agreed bank clause.

## R7. The Indian terrorism insurance pool

Source: Asia Insurance Post, GIC Re slashes premium for Terrorism Pool by 10% from Apr 1 (23 March 2025). https://asiainsurancepost.com/archives/64065
Authority: news

The Indian Market Terrorism Risk Insurance Pool (IMTRIP) covers terrorism risk written under property policies, with capacity of Rs 2,000 crore per location. With IRDAI approval its rates fell 10% for residential, non-residential and industrial risks from 1 April 2025, and earlier policies cannot be cancelled to get the lower rate. Pool premium was Rs 1,809 crore in FY23 with a claims ratio of 0.13%, and Rs 1,655 crore in FY24 with 0.21%. The pool was formed in April 2002; its members are 24 non-life insurers and GIC Re, which manages it.

## R8. National Building Code of India 2016

Source: Bureau of Indian Standards, National Building Code of India 2016 (SP 7:2016) page. https://www.bis.gov.in/standards/national-building-code/?lang=en
Authority: standard

The National Building Code is a model code for public works departments, local bodies and private builders to adopt; it binds a building only through the local authority's adoption. It covers development rules, fire and life safety (Part 4), structural design, building and plumbing services, sustainability and asset management. It was first issued in 1970 and revised in 1983, 2005 and 2016. Detailed sprinkler and hydrant requirements are in Part 4 and were not read for this note.

## R9. Fire services and fire NOCs are a State subject

Source: Press Information Bureau, Ministry of Home Affairs, Scheme for Expansion and Modernization of Fire Services (25 July 2023). https://pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=1942468
Authority: official

Fire service is a State subject and a municipal function under the Twelfth Schedule (Article 243W), so fire safety certificates and NOC rules are set by each State and city, not the Centre. The Centre keeps no central data on fire incidents in the States. A Model Bill for State Fire and Emergency Services was sent to all States and Union Territories on 16 September 2019. A Rs 5,000 crore scheme for fire services launched on 4 July 2023 runs to 2025-26, with Rs 500 crore set aside to reward States that adopt legal and infrastructure reforms.

## R10. National and State disaster risk funds

Source: Press Information Bureau, Ministry of Home Affairs, Modernization of Fire Services (1 August 2023). https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1944718
Authority: official

The Fifteenth Finance Commission set up a National and State Disaster Risk Management Fund for 2021-22 to 2025-26, with allocations of Rs 68,463 crore and Rs 1,60,153 crore, each split into response and mitigation windows. States pay relief from their State Disaster Response Fund, and the National Disaster Response Fund adds money for disasters of severe nature after a central team visits. Our reading: government relief is not insurance and does not replace a property owner's own cover.

## R11. State disaster mitigation funds

Source: Press Information Bureau, Ministry of Home Affairs, Status of State Disaster Mitigation Fund (15 March 2023). https://pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=1907163
Authority: official

The Fifteenth Finance Commission shares disaster funds using a formula of capacity (past spending), exposure (area and population) and a disaster risk index. The National Disaster Mitigation Fund was set up on 5 February 2021, and 25 States had reported setting up their own mitigation fund. The State mitigation allocation for 2021-22 to 2025-26 is Rs 32,031 crore, funded 75:25 by Centre and State, or 90:10 for North-Eastern and Himalayan States. Maharashtra had the largest allocation in the table.

## R12. India's natural catastrophe protection gap

Source: The Indian Express, reporting a Swiss Re study, Uninsured losses of $32.94 billion in India due to natural disasters in five years (17 January 2024). https://indianexpress.com/article/business/uninsured-losses-of-32-94-billion-in-india-due-to-natural-disasters-in-five-years-swiss-re-9113197/
Authority: news

Swiss Re estimated India's uninsured natural catastrophe losses at USD 32.94 billion over 2018 to 2022, against insured losses of only USD 1.10 billion, and that about 93% of India's natural catastrophe exposure is uninsured. The main perils named are earthquake, flood, tropical cyclone, drought and wildfire. Swiss Re said underwriters need finer exposure data and better catastrophe models, citing cyclone early warning as progress and floods as lagging.

## R13. Flood exposure in India

Source: National Disaster Management Authority, Floods (page last updated December 2025). https://ndma.gov.in/floods
Authority: official

Of India's 329 million hectares, more than 40 million hectares are flood prone, about one eighth of the country. Average annual flood damage was Rs 4,745 crore over 1996 to 2005, compared with Rs 1,805 crore over the 53 years before, which NDMA links to population growth, urbanisation, more economic activity in flood plains and global warming. In an average year floods affect 7.5 million hectares and kill about 1,600 people, and floods now reach areas not considered flood prone before. About 80% of rain falls from June to September, and cyclones and cloudbursts cause flash floods.

## R14. Urban flooding

Source: National Disaster Management Authority, Urban Floods (page last updated December 2025). https://ndma.gov.in/urban-floods
Authority: official

Urban catchments raise flood peaks 1.8 to 8 times and flood volumes up to 6 times, and water arrives within minutes. Older storm-water drains were designed for 12 to 20 mm of rain and are easily overwhelmed, and encroachment on natural drains and flood plains, plus waste and debris in drains, cuts their capacity. After the Mumbai floods of July 2005 NDMA treated urban flooding as a disaster separate from river floods. Listed urban floods include Hyderabad 2000, Ahmedabad 2001, Delhi 2002 to 2010, Chennai 2004, Mumbai 2005, Surat 2006, Kolkata 2007 and Guwahati 2010. Our reading: a city-centre site far from any river can still flood.

## R15. Cyclone exposure on India's coasts

Source: National Disaster Management Authority, Cyclone (page last updated December 2025). https://ndma.gov.in/cyclone
Authority: official

India has 8,041 km of coastline and faces about 10% of the world's tropical cyclones. Five or six form each year, two or three of them severe, about four in the Bay of Bengal for every one in the Arabian Sea. Between 1891 and 1990, 262 cyclones (92 severe) struck a 50 km strip of the east coast against 33 (19 severe) on the west coast. The seasons are May to June and October to November, peaking in November. Storm surge, which floods low-lying coastal land with seawater, causes the most damage. IMD classes start at depression (31 to 49 km/h); a cyclonic storm is 61 to 88 km/h, a severe cyclonic storm 88 to 117 km/h and a super cyclone above 221 km/h.

## R16. Earthquake exposure

Source: National Disaster Management Authority, Earthquakes (page last updated December 2025). https://ndma.gov.in/earthquakes
Authority: official

Over 59% of India's land is exposed to shaking of MSK intensity VII or more. The whole Himalayan belt can produce earthquakes above magnitude 8, as in Shillong 1897, Kangra 1905, Bihar-Nepal 1934 and Assam-Tibet 1950. The zone map was revised after Koyna (1967) and Killari (1993), when Zone I was merged into Zone II and parts of the Deccan moved to Zone III. North-East India averages one earthquake above magnitude 6 a year, and the Andaman and Nicobar Islands sit on a plate boundary. Industries with sensitive equipment can be disrupted even by moderate shaking.

## R17. Landslide-prone regions

Source: National Disaster Management Authority, Landslide (page last updated December 2025). https://ndma.gov.in/landslide
Authority: official

Landslides affect about 15% of India's land: the Himalayas, the North-East hills, the Western Ghats, the Nilgiris, the Eastern Ghats and the Vindhyas. The most vulnerable are the north-west and north-east Himalayas and the Western Ghats, where laterite-capped slopes above the Konkan coast and the Nilgiris fail. NDMA guidelines call for landslide hazard zonation maps at 1:50,000 scale or finer for specific sites, a task NRSC supports with remote sensing. Our reading: a hill site should get a slope and drainage check before cover is agreed.

## R18. Heat wave criteria

Source: National Disaster Management Authority, Heat Wave, quoting IMD criteria (page last updated December 2025). https://ndma.gov.in/heat-wave
Authority: official

Heat waves mostly occur from March to June, sometimes into July, mainly in north-west India. IMD considers a heat wave only once a station's maximum reaches 40 °C in the plains or 30 °C in the hills. Where the normal maximum is 40 °C or less, 5 to 6 °C above normal is a heat wave and 7 °C or more a severe one; where the normal is above 40 °C, the margins are 4 to 5 °C and 6 °C or more. A maximum of 45 °C or more is a heat wave whatever the normal. Our reading: heat wave is not a named insured property peril, but it matters for cold storage, power and fire load.

## R19. Seismic zones and high-hazard cities

Source: Press Information Bureau, Ministry of Earth Sciences, Seismic Mapping, Lok Sabha reply (30 July 2021). https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1740656
Authority: official

About 59% of India's land is earthquake prone: about 11% in Zone V, 18% in Zone IV and 30% in Zone III, with the rest in Zone II. Zone V cities include Guwahati, Srinagar, Bhuj, Imphal, Port Blair, Darbhanga and Mandi; Zone IV includes Delhi, Kolkata, Patna, Chandigarh and Dehradun. The zone map uses peak ground acceleration from 0.16 g to above 0.36 g with a 10% chance of being exceeded in 50 years. Seismic microzonation is planned for cities of 5 lakh people and more.

## R20. BMTPC Vulnerability Atlas of India

Source: Building Materials and Technology Promotion Council, Vulnerability Atlas of India, third edition (released 2 March 2019). https://bmtpc.org/topics.aspx?mid=56&Mid1=180
Authority: official

The third edition of the Vulnerability Atlas has digitised State and Union Territory maps of earthquake, wind and flood hazard, and adds thunderstorm, cyclone and landslide maps, so vulnerable areas can be found district by district. It includes district tables of housing vulnerability by wall and roof type, based on Census 2011 housing data. Our reading: it is the official multi-hazard reference for checking a portfolio district by district.

## R21. El Niño, La Niña and the monsoon

Source: IMD Climate Research and Services, Pune, Frequently Asked Questions about Long Range Forecasting (undated, data to about 2008). https://imdpune.gov.in/faq.php
Authority: official

El Niño is a warming of the central and eastern equatorial Pacific and La Niña its cold phase. IMD says the monsoon and El Niño are generally inversely related, so El Niño usually comes with a weaker monsoon, but the link is not one to one: from 1885 to 2007, rainfall was below normal in only about 4 of every 10 El Niño years. A normal monsoon is within 10% of the long-term average of 89 cm. The monsoon brings 75 to 80% of India's annual rain, and 90% or more in much of central and north-west India. Our reading: an El Niño forecast does not rule out local flood losses.

## R22. Central Water Commission flood forecasts

Source: Central Water Commission, Flood Forecasting and Hydrological Observation (page updated October 2026). https://cwc.gov.in/en/flood-forecasting-hydrological-observation
Authority: official

About 48% of India's 40 million hectares of flood-prone area has some protection, but only against low to moderate floods. CWC runs 325 forecasting stations, 197 for towns and low-lying areas and 128 for reservoir inflows, on 20 river systems, and issues over 7,000 forecasts a year with more than 90% accuracy, from May or June to October or December. Its levels are normal, above normal (yellow, at warning level), severe (orange, at danger level, updated every 3 hours) and extreme (red, at or above the highest flood level on record, updated hourly).

## R23. Storm surge risk along the coast

Source: INCOIS, Storm Surge, Indian Tsunami Early Warning System (undated). https://tsunami.incois.gov.in/TEWS/AboutStormSurge.jsp
Authority: official

Storm surge is the rise of the sea above the normal tide, driven mainly by cyclone winds. INCOIS grades coasts as very high risk (surge above 5 m), high (3 to 5 m) and moderate (1.5 to 3 m). The worst stretches are the Bengal coast and its islands (about 10 to 13 m), Paradip to Balasore in Odisha and Bapatla to Kakinada in Andhra Pradesh (about 5 to 7 m); Pamban to Nagapattinam in Tamil Nadu is high (3 to 5 m) and Gujarat moderate (2 to 3 m). Surge height depends on the storm's size, strength, speed, track, landfall point and the shape and height of the coast. Cyclones can also bring 40 to 50 cm of rain in a day.

## R24. Tsunami and high-wave warnings

Source: INCOIS, Indian Tsunami Early Warning System, About Us (undated). https://tsunami.incois.gov.in/TEWS/about.jsp
Authority: official

The Indian Tsunami Early Warning Centre at INCOIS runs around the clock and covers storm surge as well as tsunamis. It works out earthquake parameters in near real time for the two tsunami-generating zones that affect the Indian Ocean, using seabed pressure recorders, tide gauges and coastal radar, and produces coastal inundation and vulnerability maps from models. INCOIS also issues high-wave and swell-surge advisories at three levels: warning, alert and watch.

## R25. NRSC Landslide Atlas district ranking

Source: NRSC (ISRO), Landslide Atlas of India (2023, updated 25 August 2023), page 60 and Table 3. https://www.nrsc.gov.in/nrscnew/resources_atlas_landslide.php
Authority: official

NRSC built a database of landslides mapped from satellite and aerial images for 1998 to 2022 and used it to rank 147 districts in 17 States and 2 Union Territories by their exposure to landslides, measured with socio-economic factors such as total and working population, literacy and number of houses. Rudraprayag in Uttarakhand, with the highest landslide density in India, ranks first, followed by Tehri Garhwal and Thrissur. All 14 districts of Kerala are ranked, Wayanad at 13, and Mumbai Suburban and Mumbai appear at 139 and 140. The atlas uses older district boundaries, so newer districts take their parent's rank. Source: NRSC, ISRO/DOS; non-commercial use with attribution.

## R26. El Niño summers bring more extreme monsoon rain

Source: Hill, Meyers, Sobel, Biasutti, Cane, Tippett and Ahmed, More extreme Indian monsoon daily rainfall in El Niño summers, arXiv 2404.12419 (April 2024, CC BY 4.0). https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12419
Authority: research

El Niño makes dry days and dry summers more likely across India, yet data from 1901 to 2020 show it also raises the chance of extreme daily rain in monsoonal India, most of all in central-eastern India and the narrow south-west coastal strip. Extremes are suppressed in the drier south-east and far north-west. The pattern holds when El Niño is taken from a May seasonal forecast, so the signal is usable before the season.

## R27. All-India rainfall hides regional differences

Source: Hill, Sobel, Biasutti and Cane, On the all-India rainfall index and sub-India rainfall heterogeneity, arXiv 2104.12306, Geophysical Research Letters 2022 (CC BY 4.0). https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.12306
Authority: research

Using 120 years of data, the study finds the all-India rainfall index mostly tracks how widespread wet spells are and the number of rainy days, and its main pattern follows El Niño. A second pattern has three zones: the Western Ghats and the central monsoon zone move together while south-eastern India moves the opposite way. The Indian Ocean Dipole's link to the index is weak until El Niño's effect is removed. Our reading: an all-India forecast is a poor guide for a single site in Tamil Nadu or the Western Ghats.

## R28. Mumbai flood hotspots from crowdsourced data

Source: Tripathy, Chaudhuri, Murtugudde and others, Analysis of Mumbai Floods in recent Years with Crowdsourced Data, arXiv 2306.09770 (June 2023, CC BY 4.0). https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.09770
Authority: research

Mumbai has no real-time flood monitoring and few detailed records of past floods, so the authors mapped floods from location-tagged social media posts and checked them against volunteered reports and a height-above-drainage map. Extreme rain events are becoming more frequent, but recent flood reports have fallen, which local surveys credit to underground storage tanks and pumping stations at hotspots. Our reading: site-level drainage works can matter as much as the city's rainfall.

## R29. Landslide susceptibility in Kerala's Western Ghats

Source: Achu, Aju and Reghunath, Spatial modelling of shallow landslide susceptibility: a study from the southern Western Ghats region of Kerala, India, Annals of GIS 26(2), 2020 (open access). https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/19475683.2020.1758207
Authority: research

In a 720 km2 study area across Kozhikode and Wayanad with 82 mapped landslides, about 74% of landslides were on slopes of 10 to 30 degrees and none above 40 degrees, where the ground is bare rock. In the August 2018 deluge 61% of landslides were on farmland with 10 to 40 degree slopes, and 17 recent landslides were within 300 m of a road. The best model placed about 86% of the 2018 landslides in its high or extreme susceptibility classes. Our reading: moderate slopes, road cuts and cultivated hillsides are the warning signs for a hill site.

## R30. Cyclone losses rising in the North Indian Ocean

Source: Yadav and Das, Analysis of the Impact of North Indian Ocean Cyclonic Disturbance on Human and Economic Losses, arXiv 2306.07555, Revista de Climatología 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07555
Authority: research

Looking at North Indian Ocean cyclonic disturbances from 1990 to 2022, the authors find their number trending upward. Deaths have fallen slightly since 2015, which they credit to better disaster preparedness, but economic losses have risen considerably because of fast economic growth in exposed areas. Our reading: fewer deaths do not mean smaller property losses.

## R31. Rapid intensification of Cyclone Amphan

Source: Balasubramanian and Chalamalla, Super Cyclone Amphan: A Dynamical Case Study, arXiv 2007.02982 (November 2020). https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.02982
Authority: research

Amphan was the first super cyclone in the Bay of Bengal in 21 years. It grew from Category 1 to Category 5 in under 36 hours and made landfall in West Bengal as a very severe cyclonic storm with winds near 155 km/h. The authors link the rapid growth to an upper-atmosphere Kelvin wave coupling with the Madden-Julian Oscillation. Our reading: warning time before a Bay of Bengal storm escalates can be short.

## R32. Arabian Sea marine heatwaves and extreme rain

Source: Suhas, Han, Shinoda and others, Marine Heatwaves in the Arabian Sea: Drivers and Impacts on Atmospheric Circulation and Extreme Precipitation, arXiv 2603.18319 (March 2026, preprint under review). https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18319
Authority: research

The eastern and northern Arabian Sea are marine heatwave hotspots, and heatwaves there rose rapidly over 1982 to 2023, mostly because of human-caused warming. Most last 20 days or less, and El Niño and the Indian Ocean Dipole change how many occur. Eastern Arabian Sea heatwaves raise the risk of extreme rain on India's south-west coast, and northern ones feed cyclones that bring heavy rain to north-west India and Pakistan. This is a preprint, not yet peer reviewed.

## R33. Site-specific seismic hazard in Tripura

Source: Sinha and Selvan, An improved Probabilistic Seismic Hazard Assessment of Tripura, India, arXiv 2212.11605, Pure and Applied Geophysics 2022 (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0). https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.11605
Authority: research

North-East India is one of the most seismically active regions in the world. The study models earthquake sources in three depth layers (0 to 25, 25 to 70 and 70 to 180 km) and maps peak and spectral ground acceleration on a 0.05 degree grid for 10% and 2% chances of being exceeded in 50 years. Our reading: site-specific hazard can differ from the single value implied by a seismic zone label.

## R34. Basis risk in weather index insurance

Source: Michler, Viens and Shively, Risk, Agricultural Production, and Weather Index Insurance in Village India, arXiv 2103.11047, Journal of the Agricultural and Applied Economics Association 2022 (CC BY 4.0). https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.11047
Authority: research

In parcel-level data from Indian villages, seasonal weather explains only 19 to 20% of the variation in crop yields. The authors derive fair prices and payout schedules for index insurance, and their results help explain why uptake is low. Our reading: when most loss is not explained by the index, a parametric payout can differ a lot from the actual loss.

## R35. Chennai floods 2015: claims

Source: Business Standard, Chennai floods insurance claims touch Rs 4,800 crore (22 January 2016). https://www.business-standard.com/article/current-affairs/chennai-floods-insurance-claims-touch-rs-4-800-crore-116012201026_1.html
Authority: news

Insurers faced about Rs 4,800 crore of claims from the 2015 Chennai floods, with public sector insurers hit hardest. New India's chairman put the number of claims at about 50,000: motor had the most claims and property the highest value. Corporate all-risk policies, including production-interruption cover, drew claims because factories and offices stayed under water for more than 3 days. Experts said insured losses were low because few victims had insurance.

## R36. Chennai floods 2015: two waves

Source: The Economic Times (PTI), Chennai flood claims at Rs 5,000 crore for insurance sector (8 March 2016). https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/chennai-flood-claims-at-rs-5000-crore-for-insurance-sector/articleshow/51316134.cms
Authority: news

National Insurance's chairman put industry flood losses at about Rs 5,000 crore. After the November flood the industry expected no more than Rs 500 crore; the second flood in December did the major damage. About 80,000 vehicles were estimated to be damaged. Our reading: two waves weeks apart raise the question of whether a policy's hours clause treats them as one event or two.

## R37. Cyclone Michaung 2023: fewer claims than 2015

Source: Business Standard, Insurance claims drop 30-35% during Chennai floods compared to 2015 (24 December 2023, partly paywalled). https://www.business-standard.com/finance/insurance/insurance-claims-drop-30-35-during-chennai-floods-compared-to-2015-123122400503_1.html
Authority: news

Cyclone Michaung brought Chennai its heaviest rain in 47 years in December 2023, yet industry sources reported 30 to 35% fewer motor and non-motor claims than in 2015, crediting quick action by the authorities and the public. General insurers issued 74% more policies in 2021-22 than in 2014-15. Our reading: preparedness can cut losses even in a larger rain event.

## R38. Cyclone Michaung 2023: claim camps for industry

Source: Business Standard (PTI), Special camps to settle insurance claims once flood water recedes in TN: FM (22 December 2023). https://www.business-standard.com/india-news/special-camps-to-settle-insurance-claims-once-flood-water-recedes-in-tn-fm-123122200602_1.html
Authority: news

Nineteen insurers under the General Insurance Council held a claims camp at Ambattur Industrial Estate on 20 and 21 December 2023 and settled claims on the spot after surveying affected units. A separate heavy-rain event on 16 to 19 December hit Tirunelveli, Thoothukudi, Kanyakumari and Tenkasi, and the Centre released Rs 900 crore as its share of the State Disaster Response Fund.

## R39. Cyclone Biparjoy 2023 in Gujarat

## R41. Cyclone Amphan 2020: claimed versus paid

Source: Down To Earth, Indian insurers listed among worst performers in climate sector globally (31 March 2022). https://www.downtoearth.org.in/climate-change/indian-insurers-listed-among-worst-performers-in-climate-sector-globally-82166
Authority: news

Citing an IRDAI report, the article says Amphan produced 14,575 claims worth Rs 1,767 crore in 2020-21, and 11,512 were settled by 30 June 2021 for Rs 471 crore, the highest payout for any weather event that year. An expert quoted said about 80% of claims were settled but only about 26% of the money claimed was paid, and that about 70% of Rs 2,559 crore of catastrophe claims in 2020-21 were unpaid. The article gives Amphan's date as May 2021; the cyclone struck in May 2020. Our reading: underinsurance, deductibles and exclusions can leave a large gap between claimed and paid amounts.
