# Underwriting Guidelines & Appetite

Sections G1 to G6 are the capstone team's original guidelines (underwriting_guidelines.pdf). Sections G7 to G12 restate rules the scoring engine already applies, added so retrieval covers every scored factor. All sections are prototype guidance, not filed insurance rating rules.

## G1. Construction & Occupancy Risk

Frame construction and higher-hazard occupancies such as Warehouse and Industrial merit additional underwriting scrutiny. Buildings without sprinkler coverage in these occupancies warrant elevated risk consideration. The presence or absence of active fire suppression systems directly impacts the underwriting decision. Properties with combustible framing in high-value occupancies require careful evaluation of replacement cost and loss potential.

## G2. Roof Age Thresholds

Roofs older than 20 years are considered aging and may trigger referral reviews. Roofs older than 30 years warrant stronger concern and are likely to require replacement planning or mitigation before renewal. The age of the roof significantly affects both loss frequency and severity. Modern roofing materials provide better wind and weather resistance compared to aged materials. Documentation of recent roof maintenance or replacement is favorable for underwriting decisions.

## G3. CAT Zone Appetite

Exposure in wildfire, flood, or wind zones materially increases risk and is not generally considered low risk. Proximity to coast or interface areas compounds this concern. Properties located within designated catastrophe exposure zones require enhanced scrutiny and may command higher premiums or stricter underwriting requirements. Natural disaster exposure is a primary driver of overall risk classification.

## G4. Prior Loss History Appetite

Properties with more than two prior claims in five years suggest a negative loss pattern and should be reviewed for adverse loss history and pricing implications. Frequency of losses is more concerning than severity in many cases. A history of multiple claims indicates potential maintenance issues, operational risks, or problematic property conditions that warrant close attention during underwriting.

## G5. TIV Concentration Limits

Total insured value above USD 20 million requires enhanced risk review because of concentration exposure and more significant loss severity. Large single-location concentrations of value create potential for catastrophic loss. Properties with very high TIV require additional scrutiny regarding building condition, occupancy hazards, and business continuity considerations.

## G6. Referral vs Decline Criteria

Scores from 0 to 30 are generally acceptable for approval. Scores 31 to 60 warrant referral for further review by senior underwriters. Scores 61 to 84 are decline candidates where mitigation measures may reduce the exposure and allow for reconsideration. Scores 85 to 100 are auto-decline based on the combined risk profile and require exceptional circumstances for override. The system uses deterministic underwriting rules to produce a score from 0 to 100, with additive rules applied consistently across all applications.

## G7. Seismic Zones

India's seismic zoning map (BIS IS 1893) places sites in zones II to V. Properties in zones IV and V carry high earthquake shaking hazard and add to the risk score; zones II and III are captured but not scored in this prototype. Structural design, building height and construction type should be reviewed for high-seismic sites, and earthquake cover requests should be checked against the declared zone.

## G8. Coastal and Wildland Proximity

A site less than one mile from the coast is exposed to wind and storm-surge losses, and a site less than one mile from a fire zone sits in the wildland-urban interface. Each condition adds to the risk score independently of the CAT zone. Distances must come from the submission; a missing distance is treated as unknown, never as safe.

## G9. Protection and Mitigation

Automatic sprinklers, fire alarms and flood protection reduce expected loss and are shown as indicative mitigation benefits. Flood protection only counts where the property is in a flood zone. Generators, drainage and security safeguards are recorded but carry no scored benefit. Mitigation benefits inform the indicative risk view and referral discussions; they never change the authoritative deterministic score.

## G10. Product Segments

Indicative product segment follows total value at risk: up to INR 5 crore (50 million) maps to Bharat Sookshma Udyam Suraksha, up to INR 50 crore (500 million) to Bharat Laghu Udyam Suraksha, and anything larger to the larger-risk commercial property segment. Total value at risk is the sum of building, plant and machinery, furniture and fixtures, stock and other contents values.

## G11. Coverage Extensions

Requested extensions such as RSMD, flood and STFI, cyclone and wind, earthquake, terrorism and business interruption describe what the applicant wants insured. A requested extension is not evidence of exposure to that peril and must not be turned into a risk factor. Review applicability under the selected product, dependencies between extensions, and any exclusions or deductibles that need confirmation.

## G12. Image and Inspection Evidence

Property photos are analysed for visible roof condition, structural damage, vegetation and defensible space, maintenance level and visible hazards. Image observations are supporting evidence only: they never overwrite declared facts such as construction type, sprinkler status or roof age, and an image with no substantive observations is treated as unusable.
